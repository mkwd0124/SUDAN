Sudan

A land of the Nile and ancient civilizations, from desert pyramids to Red Sea reefs and the highlands of Darfur.

Start the journey

Six things that define Sudan

Sudan combines a great river, ancient history, varied nature and national symbols. Explore the places and the emblem that tell its story.

The River Nile

Flows through the whole country, from south to north

The Nile is the lifeline of Sudan. The Blue Nile and the White Nile meet in Khartoum and continue north as one river, giving water to farms, towns and ancient cities along its banks.

  • The meeting of the two Niles is at Al-Mogran in Khartoum.
  • Many of Sudan's ancient sites were built beside the river.
The River Nile

Bajrawiya Pyramids

North of Khartoum, near the River Nile

These are the burial places of the kings and queens of the Kingdom of Kush, in the ancient city of Meroë. They have steep sides and small bases compared with the pyramids of Egypt, and there are more than 200 of them.

  • Listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
  • Include temples and carvings that mix Kushite and Egyptian culture.
Bajrawiya Pyramids, Meroë

Coral Reefs of Port Sudan

Red Sea coast

The Red Sea reefs off Port Sudan are among the best diving spots in the world, with clear water, colorful coral and a wide variety of fish.

  • Famous sites include Sanganeb and Dungonab.
  • Great for diving, snorkeling and underwater photography.
Red Sea coral reef

Jebel Marra

Darfur region, western Sudan

A volcanic mountain range that rises to about 3,000 meters. The climate is mild and relatively cool, with green farmland, springs and waterfalls.

  • The Deriba Caldera and its two lakes inside the crater.
  • Good for camping and nature walks.
Jebel Marra highlands

Al-Fateh Tower

Khartoum, on the bank of the Blue Nile

One of the best-known landmarks of the modern capital, shaped like a large sail. It overlooks the Nile and is one of the most recognizable buildings in Khartoum.

  • Also known as the Corinthia Hotel.
  • A good spot to watch the city and the Nile at sunset.
Al-Fateh Tower, Khartoum

Saqr al-Jadiyan

The national emblem of Sudan

Saqr al-Jadiyan is the secretary bird shown on Sudan's national emblem. The bird is known for its long legs and its strength, and it stands as a symbol of the country.

  • The emblem shows the bird with a shield and a scroll.
  • It appears on official documents and government buildings.
